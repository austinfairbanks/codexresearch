## What are the five highest-visibility research-use-only peptides in the United States, and what does credible evidence show about effects, harms, laboratory sourcing and pricing, and ongoing research?

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_a88de91015b8464c84f8617a11607c0c -->

### How this top-five list should be read

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_5f6aa46194f7039ec47adccd711e79c7 -->

There is no audited public U.S. dataset of RUO peptide sales, so this is a market-visibility shortlist—not a verified unit-sales ranking. It covers compounds recurring across recent peer-reviewed reviews, FDA safety materials, public RUO catalogs, and registry searches. FDA and peer-reviewed human evidence receive the most weight; animal and in-vitro results remain hypotheses about human benefit. ClinicalTrials.gov records are sponsor-submitted, and public catalog pages are used only for dated price observations. This project provides no human-use, administration, or dosing guidance.

### 1. BPC-157

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_61c363f5677a9ec89da484fbcef46ce6 -->

#### Main effects

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_cc74bc01a0e81b1edca1870c963b3449 -->

Research and marketing center on tendon, muscle, gastrointestinal, and wound repair, with proposed angiogenesis, nitric-oxide, fibroblast, and tissue-healing mechanisms. Most efficacy evidence remains animal or laboratory work; human therapeutic efficacy is not established.

#### Side effects

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_b73357674ccee23ff4bbfa68f5bd2a5c -->

FDA flags immunogenicity, aggregation, peptide impurities, and API-characterization concerns and finds safety information insufficient for proposed routes. The human adverse-event profile is largely unknown. Angiogenesis or tumor concerns are theoretical, not demonstrated clinical harms.

#### Sourcing & pricing

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_a5845c3417745614ccde7cfce3a0902b -->

Public research-quantity listings observed July 25–26, 2026 showed 5 mg at $52 and 10 mg around $92–$97. These prices are benchmarks only. Legitimate research procurement should require lot identity, chain of custody, LC-MS/HPLC evidence, and experiment-appropriate contaminant testing.

#### Ongoing research

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_7e00a34780a0b34c070b4c0f636ffd21 -->

NCT07437547 is a recruiting sponsor-submitted Phase 2 hamstring-strain record. NCT02637284 is an older Phase 1 record with unknown status and no posted results. Registration does not validate efficacy, safety, or legality.

### 2. TB-500 (thymosin beta-4 fragment)

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_1fccf76ca341b71438289d31dd85082e -->

#### Main effects

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_86fd33827b3f9ac61d80742d7f6b0394 -->

Marketing emphasizes tissue repair, mobility, cell migration, and angiogenesis. RUO TB-500 is a short thymosin beta-4 fragment; research on full-length thymosin beta-4 cannot be transferred to the fragment. Direct human evidence for the fragment is absent.

#### Side effects

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_f598716131282f72fff1e1f3a233dab3 -->

FDA reports no human exposure data for the TB-500 fragment and flags immunogenicity, aggregation, and peptide-impurity risks. Its human adverse-event profile is unknown.

#### Sourcing & pricing

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_c759e7fd0b0e7c79531dda243269e611 -->

Public 5 mg listings observed July 25–26, 2026 ranged from $72 to $78. A product labeled TB-500 should be verified against the actual fragment sequence rather than assumed to be full-length thymosin beta-4.

#### Ongoing research

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_5a337108c4481adb55b7a6cc4e711f64 -->

The NCT07487363 search result explicitly describes itself as a fictional example, so it is excluded as evidence of a real program. Full-length thymosin beta-4 trials are related biology, not direct evidence for TB-500.

### 3. CJC-1295

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_e43a09828502efe036e6cc56f850307f -->

#### Main effects

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_5a0d197512e8563ae94a4e5c91bbe82e -->

CJC-1295 is a long-acting growth-hormone-releasing-hormone analog. A randomized healthy-adult study demonstrated prolonged growth-hormone and IGF-1 increases, establishing pharmacodynamic activity—not proven recovery, body-composition, or performance benefits.

#### Side effects

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_ac097f2bc1513e853c122311bc426140 -->

FDA notes limited clinical data and serious events including increased heart rate and a systemic vasodilatory reaction. Plausible growth-axis concerns include headache, flushing, edema, altered glucose handling, and cardiovascular effects, but RUO incidence is not established.

#### Sourcing & pricing

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_2e2b54b173e3b878cad7e00a60da5c31 -->

CJC-1295 without DAC, 5 mg, was observed at $41 from one public catalog. DAC and no-DAC forms are not interchangeable; identity testing must resolve the actual molecule.

#### Ongoing research

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_5058c2886d750af25093100d86b278dd -->

NCT00267527, a Phase 2 program in HIV-associated visceral obesity, was terminated. No active credible interventional program was identified in the registry search.

### 4. Ipamorelin

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_e56d39334a6d44572ea56e44be61fef9 -->

#### Main effects

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_39ffcbbb0d119529047dcf5c921fb66d -->

Ipamorelin is a ghrelin-receptor growth-hormone secretagogue studied for growth-hormone release and gastrointestinal motility. A randomized Phase 2 postoperative-ileus study provides human context but does not validate broad recovery or performance claims.

#### Side effects

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_6863e59481467a60edc756eec43659c8 -->

FDA reports serious adverse events, including death, in a literature study using intravenous ipamorelin for gastric motility and says evidence is insufficient for other injectable routes. It also flags immunogenicity, aggregation, impurities, and unnatural-amino-acid concerns.

#### Sourcing & pricing

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_1dd00006e3e4500d10a3b4d0198ef5ad -->

Public 5 mg listings observed July 25–26, 2026 were approximately $43–$53, including a displayed sale price of $46. List price is not a quality metric.

#### Ongoing research

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_6dd2853b93da5b9e5a3b45ce7c65751c -->

NCT00672074 and NCT01280344 are completed interventional records. No active interventional program clearly testing ipamorelin was identified.

### 5. GHK-Cu

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_89f3097df0bc4aeb42c110c127354e5f -->

#### Main effects

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_3e8db61c848077287e8e812a9013dca9 -->

This endogenous copper-binding tripeptide is studied for matrix remodeling, collagen-related signaling, wound repair, and skin biology. Positive work is largely preclinical or topical/cosmetic and does not establish systemic or injectable benefit.

#### Side effects

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_a4b61030e24759870993bdfc5571f947 -->

FDA says human safety information for injectable GHK-Cu is limited and flags immunogenicity, aggregation, and peptide-impurity concerns. Topical irritation is plausible; systemic copper and injectable risks remain poorly characterized.

#### Sourcing & pricing

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_e6a52e678e43215eb2c3e03e5e7f4075 -->

Public 50 mg listings observed July 25–26, 2026 ranged from $58 to $64. Copper loading, identity, purity, and stability should be analytically confirmed for the intended experiment.

#### Ongoing research

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_0e466cacf239b4bb02f8aafb9ba45205 -->

Registry searches returned NCT07706361, a not-yet-recruiting patch study, and NCT07437586, a recruiting topical wound-healing record. Both are sponsor-submitted and neither supports injectable RUO use.

### What remains unknown

<!-- soleresearch:node nod_c01cabfe0b50aa81307221d009ac9418 -->

A true U.S. popularity ranking requires audited sales or procurement data that are not public. For all five compounds, major gaps include independently replicated human efficacy, route-specific safety, long-term adverse events, validated dosing, interaction data, and reliable post-market surveillance. Future updates should re-check primary publications and verify suspicious or newly posted registry entries before changing conclusions.
