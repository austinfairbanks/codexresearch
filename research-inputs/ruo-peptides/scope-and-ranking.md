# Scope and ranking method

## Research question

Which five research-use-only peptides have the highest current visibility in the United States, and what does credible evidence say about their main effects, side effects, laboratory sourcing and pricing, and ongoing research?

## Scope boundary

This project covers compounds sold or discussed as research-use-only (RUO), not FDA-approved peptide medicines. It does not recommend human use, dosing, administration, or vendors. “Research use only” is a seller label, not evidence of identity, purity, efficacy, or safety.

## Ranking limitation

There is no audited public national dataset of U.S. RUO peptide unit sales. Accordingly, “top five” is a transparent market-visibility shortlist rather than a verified sales ranking. The shortlist is BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, and GHK-Cu, chosen because they recur across recent peer-reviewed reviews of the unapproved peptide market, FDA safety-risk materials, public RUO catalogs, and current research-registry searches.

## Evidence hierarchy

FDA safety communications and peer-reviewed human studies receive the most weight. Animal and in-vitro studies can support mechanisms or research hypotheses but not human efficacy. ClinicalTrials.gov records show registration status only; they are sponsor-submitted and do not prove that a treatment works, is safe, or is legally marketed. Vendor pages are used only for dated, directly observed price snapshots and never as scientific or quality evidence.

## Key review

A 2026 Sports Medicine review describes a gray market of unapproved peptides outside normal regulatory oversight and includes BPC-157, CJC-1295, GHK-Cu, ipamorelin, thymosin beta-4, and TB-500. It concludes that favorable findings are often preclinical while rigorous human safety data are scarce and serious harm is possible.
