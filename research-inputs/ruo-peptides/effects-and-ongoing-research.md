# Effects, evidence limits, and ongoing research

## BPC-157

Main research themes are tendon, muscle, gastrointestinal, and wound repair, with proposed angiogenesis, nitric-oxide, fibroblast, and tissue-healing mechanisms. Most efficacy evidence is from animal or laboratory models; human therapeutic efficacy remains unestablished. A recruiting sponsor-submitted Phase 2 hamstring-strain record, NCT07437547, appeared in 2026, while the older Phase 1 record NCT02637284 has unknown status and no posted results. Registry presence is not validation.

Sources: PubMed PMID 40789979, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40789979/ ; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07437547, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07437547 ; NCT02637284, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02637284

## TB-500

TB-500 is marketed around tissue repair, mobility, cell migration, and angiogenesis, but the RUO product is a short thymosin beta-4 fragment. FDA found no human exposure data for this fragment. A search result labeled NCT07487363 explicitly describes itself as a fictional example, so it is excluded as evidence of an ongoing clinical program. Studies of full-length thymosin beta-4 are related biology, not direct TB-500 evidence.

Sources: FDA safety page; ClinicalTrials.gov search record NCT07487363, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07487363

## CJC-1295

CJC-1295 is a long-acting growth-hormone-releasing-hormone analog. A randomized healthy-adult study showed prolonged increases in growth hormone and IGF-1, which establishes pharmacodynamic activity rather than a proven recovery, body-composition, or performance benefit. The only located ClinicalTrials.gov program, NCT00267527 for HIV-associated visceral obesity, was terminated; no active credible interventional program was identified in the search.

Sources: PubMed PMID 16352683, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16352683/ ; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00267527, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00267527

## Ipamorelin

Ipamorelin is a ghrelin-receptor growth-hormone secretagogue studied for growth-hormone release and gastrointestinal motility. A randomized Phase 2 postoperative-ileus study supplies human clinical context but does not establish broad recovery or performance claims. Searches located completed interventional records NCT00672074 and NCT01280344, but no active interventional program clearly testing ipamorelin.

Sources: PubMed PMID 25331030, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25331030/ ; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00672074, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00672074 ; NCT01280344, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01280344

## GHK-Cu

GHK-Cu is an endogenous copper-binding tripeptide studied for matrix remodeling, collagen-related signaling, wound repair, and skin biology. Much of the positive literature is preclinical or topical/cosmetic and does not establish systemic or injectable benefit. Current registry searches returned a not-yet-recruiting patch study NCT07706361 and a recruiting topical wound-healing record NCT07437586; both should be independently scrutinized because registry entries are sponsor-submitted. These are not evidence for injectable RUO use.

Sources: PubMed PMID 29986520, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29986520/ ; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07706361, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07706361 ; NCT07437586, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07437586

## Cross-cutting interpretation

For growth-hormone-axis compounds, plausible risks include altered glucose handling, edema, headache, flushing, and cardiovascular effects, but compound-specific incidence is not well established in RUO use. For BPC-157 and TB-500, the human adverse-event profile is largely unknown. Angiogenesis or tumor-related concerns are theoretical and should not be presented as demonstrated clinical harms. Topical irritation is plausible for GHK-Cu, while systemic copper and injectable risks remain poorly characterized.
