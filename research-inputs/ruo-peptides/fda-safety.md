# FDA safety findings for the five-compound shortlist

The FDA page “Certain Bulk Drug Substances for Use in Compounding that May Present Significant Safety Risks,” updated in April 2026, is the primary regulatory safety source for this project.

## BPC-157

FDA identifies potential immunogenicity, aggregation, peptide-impurity, and active-ingredient characterization concerns. It found no or only limited safety information for proposed routes and concluded that the available information is insufficient to know whether the compound would harm humans.

## TB-500

FDA addresses the thymosin beta-4 fragment with sequence LKKTETQ, commonly called TB-500. It identifies potential immunogenicity, aggregation, and peptide-impurity concerns, found no human exposure data, and states that it lacks enough safety information to know whether the fragment would harm humans. Evidence for full-length thymosin beta-4 must not be attributed to this fragment.

## CJC-1295

FDA identifies immunogenicity, peptide-impurity, and active-ingredient characterization concerns. It notes reported serious adverse events including increased heart rate and a systemic vasodilatory reaction, and says the clinical data are limited.

## Ipamorelin acetate

FDA identifies immunogenicity, aggregation, impurity, and active-ingredient characterization concerns, including issues associated with unnatural amino acids. FDA reports that a literature study using intravenous ipamorelin for gastric motility included serious adverse events, including death, and states that information is insufficient for other injectable routes.

## Injectable GHK-Cu

FDA identifies potential immunogenicity, aggregation, and peptide-impurity concerns and says human safety information for injectable GHK-Cu is limited. Evidence from topical cosmetic use does not establish injectable safety.

Primary source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/certain-bulk-drug-substances-use-compounding-may-present-significant-safety-risks
